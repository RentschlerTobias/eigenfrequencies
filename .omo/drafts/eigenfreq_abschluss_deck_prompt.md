### Slide 3 — Why Eigenfrequencies Matter

- Turbine runner is excited by the **blade-passing frequency** of the guide vanes: f_bp = Z · n / 60.
- These excitation frequencies are **kinematic** — fully determined by vane count Z and rotation speed n. They do not require CFD to compute.
- Resonance occurs when a structural eigenfrequency falls inside an excitation band. → The optimizer must **keep modal frequencies out of forbidden bands**, alongside the hydraulic objectives.
- Role of the (still) fluid:
    - **Added mass**: still water lowers wet modes vs dry modes by 20–40% (geometry-dependent).
    - **Pre-stress**: minor effect on eigenfrequencies.
    - A static fluid has no resonance of its own; resonance requires time-varying excitation.
- Full fluid–structure interaction (Coupled Helmholtz-acoustic ↔ elasticity) is the long-term goal; amplitudes and damping come later. This exchange delivers the **dry-mode foundation**.

### Slide 7 — Coupled Objective Function

**Blade-passing band (kinematic):**
- Z = 18 guide vanes, n = 90 rpm → **f_bp = Z · n / 60 = 18 · 90 / 60 = 27 Hz**
- 7 excitation bands h = 1..7: band h = h · f_bp ± margin, **margin = max(5 Hz, 5%)**

**Resonance penalty** (soft / hard alternatives):
- Soft: penalty = k · Σᵢ max(0, lo_h − f_i, f_i − hi_h)
- Hard: 1e6 · penalty when any f_i falls inside a band

**Total objective:**

$$f_{total} = f_{CFD} + f_{resonance}$$

$$f_{CFD} = w_\eta \cdot \tanh|1+\eta| + w_{cav}\cdot \tanh(V_{cav}\cdot 10^6) + w_{head}\cdot \tanh|dH - dH_{target}|$$

- Eigenfrequency objective enters as a **penalty / constraint**, not a separate Pareto axis — keeps the hydraulic objective space low-dimensional.
